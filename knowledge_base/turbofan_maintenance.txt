TURBOFAN ENGINE DEGRADATION AND MAINTENANCE REFERENCE
Predictive Maintenance Knowledge Base
Revision 3.1

================================================================================
SECTION 1: SCOPE
================================================================================

This reference describes the degradation modes observable in turbofan engines
and the maintenance actions associated with each. It is the retrieval corpus
for the AeroPredict maintenance-report assistant.

The engine model used for validation is a two-spool turbofan with a fan, a
low-pressure compressor (LPC), a high-pressure compressor (HPC), a combustor,
a high-pressure turbine (HPT), a low-pressure turbine (LPT), and a nozzle.
Sensor channels record temperature, pressure, fan speed, core speed, fuel
flow, and bleed enthalpy.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: SENSOR CHANNELS
================================================================================

The following channels are recorded per operating cycle.

  Channel   Quantity                          Units
  --------  --------------------------------  ---------
  T2        Fan inlet total temperature        deg R
  T24       LPC outlet total temperature       deg R
  T30       HPC outlet total temperature       deg R
  T50       LPT outlet total temperature       deg R
  P2        Fan inlet pressure                 psia
  P15       Bypass duct pressure               psia
  P30       HPC outlet pressure                psia
  Nf        Physical fan speed                 rpm
  Nc        Physical core speed                rpm
  epr       Engine pressure ratio              dimensionless
  Ps30      HPC outlet static pressure          psia
  phi       Fuel flow ratio                    pps/psi
  NRf       Corrected fan speed                rpm
  NRc       Corrected core speed               rpm
  BPR       Bypass ratio                       dimensionless
  farB      Burner fuel-air ratio              dimensionless
  htBleed   Bleed enthalpy                     dimensionless
  Nf_dmd    Demanded fan speed                 rpm
  PCNfR_dmd Demanded corrected fan speed       rpm
  W31       HPT coolant bleed                  lbm/s
  W32       LPT coolant bleed                  lbm/s

================================================================================
SECTION 3: DEGRADATION MODES
================================================================================

3.1 HPC EFFICIENCY LOSS
    The high-pressure compressor loses aerodynamic efficiency as blade tip
    clearances grow and blade surfaces foul. Observable signature:
      - T30 rises relative to P30 at constant corrected core speed.
      - Ps30 falls for a given fuel flow.
      - Fuel flow ratio phi increases to hold thrust.
    Typical onset: 60 to 80 percent of useful life consumed.
    Primary indicator channels: T30, P30, Ps30, phi.

3.2 HPC FLOW LOSS
    The high-pressure compressor passes less mass flow than nominal, usually
    from blade erosion or seal wear. Observable signature:
      - P30 falls more sharply than T30 rises.
      - epr decreases.
      - Bypass ratio BPR increases slightly as core flow drops.
    Primary indicator channels: P30, epr, BPR, Nc.

3.3 FAN EFFICIENCY LOSS
    Fan blade fouling or erosion reduces the work the fan transfers to the
    bypass stream. Observable signature:
      - T24 rises relative to T2.
      - P15 falls relative to P2.
      - Corrected fan speed NRf drifts from demanded speed Nf_dmd.
    Primary indicator channels: T24, P15, NRf, Nf_dmd.

3.4 FAN FLOW LOSS
    Reduced fan mass flow, typically from foreign object damage or tip rub.
    Observable signature:
      - BPR decreases.
      - P2 to P15 pressure ratio changes.
      - Fan speed must increase to hold thrust.
    Primary indicator channels: BPR, P2, P15, Nf.

3.5 LPT EFFICIENCY LOSS
    Low-pressure turbine efficiency degrades from blade creep and tip
    clearance growth. Observable signature:
      - T50 rises for a given Nf.
      - LPT coolant bleed W32 demand increases.
    Primary indicator channels: T50, W32, Nf.

3.6 HPT EFFICIENCY LOSS
    High-pressure turbine degradation, usually the most safety-critical mode
    because it directly affects core power extraction. Observable signature:
      - T50 rises sharply.
      - HPT coolant bleed W31 demand increases.
      - Core speed Nc becomes harder to hold.
    Primary indicator channels: T50, W31, Nc, T30.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: REMAINING USEFUL LIFE
================================================================================

4.1 DEFINITION
    Remaining Useful Life (RUL) is the number of operating cycles remaining
    before the engine reaches the failure threshold. In the reference dataset
    the failure threshold is a 30 percent drop in HPC outlet static pressure
    Ps30 from its nominal value.

4.2 HEALTH INDEX
    The health index h(t) is the minimum of the normalised subsystem margins.
    The subsystem with the smallest margin is the bottleneck and determines
    the health index. A health index of 1.0 indicates a new engine; 0.0
    indicates the failure threshold.

4.3 RUL BANDS AND URGENCY
    The following bands map a predicted RUL to a maintenance urgency. These
    are the thresholds the reporting assistant should apply.

      RUL range (cycles)   Band        Urgency   Action window
      -------------------  ----------  --------  ---------------------------
      greater than 125     Nominal     Low       Routine scheduled interval
      80 to 125            Watch       Low       Plan at next scheduled check
      50 to 79             Elevated    Medium    Schedule within 10 cycles
      25 to 49             High        High      Schedule within 5 cycles
      less than 25         Critical    High      Immediate inspection

    A prediction below 25 cycles is treated as critical regardless of model
    confidence, because the cost of a missed failure exceeds the cost of an
    unnecessary inspection.

================================================================================
SECTION 5: MAINTENANCE ACTIONS BY MODE
================================================================================

5.1 HPC EFFICIENCY LOSS - ACTION
    Perform a compressor water wash. If efficiency does not recover above the
    nominal band, borescope the HPC stages and measure tip clearance. Replace
    blades if clearance exceeds the service limit.

5.2 HPC FLOW LOSS - ACTION
    Borescope the HPC for blade erosion and seal wear. Check the variable
    stator vane schedule. Replace worn seals. If flow loss exceeds 3 percent,
    remove the compressor module for overhaul.

5.3 FAN EFFICIENCY LOSS - ACTION
    Inspect fan blades for fouling and erosion. Clean blades and rebalance the
    fan rotor. Check the fan case for rub marks.

5.4 FAN FLOW LOSS - ACTION
    Perform a fan blade tip clearance inspection. Check for foreign object
    damage on the leading edges. Replace damaged blades as a matched set to
    preserve rotor balance.

5.5 LPT EFFICIENCY LOSS - ACTION
    Borescope the LPT stages. Measure tip clearance. Check the LPT coolant
    bleed schedule against the nominal curve.

5.6 HPT EFFICIENCY LOSS - ACTION
    This is the highest-priority mode. Borescope the HPT immediately. Inspect
    for blade creep, coating loss, and cooling hole blockage. Verify the HPT
    coolant bleed W31 is within schedule. Any HPT anomaly with a predicted RUL
    below 50 cycles requires an immediate inspection.

================================================================================
SECTION 6: DIAGNOSTIC PROCEDURE
================================================================================

When a predicted RUL falls into the Elevated band or worse, run the following
sequence.

  Step 1  Compare the current cycle's sensor vector against the nominal
          baseline for the same corrected core speed.
  Step 2  Identify which channels deviate by more than two standard
          deviations. Map those channels to a degradation mode using
          Section 3.
  Step 3  Confirm the mode with a second, independent channel from the same
          mode's indicator list. A single-channel deviation is not sufficient
          evidence.  Step 4  Apply the maintenance action from Section 5 for the confirmed mode.
  Step 5  Record the finding, the confirmed mode, and the action taken in the
          engine log.

================================================================================
SECTION 7: REPORTING REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

Every generated maintenance report must state:

  1. The predicted RUL in cycles and the corresponding urgency band.
  2. The most likely degradation mode, with the specific sensor channels that
     support it.
  3. The recommended maintenance action from Section 5.
  4. An explicit statement of uncertainty. If the model's prediction is close
     to a band boundary, say so rather than asserting a single band.

Reports must not assert a degradation mode that is not supported by at least
two indicator channels. When the evidence is ambiguous, the report must say
that the evidence is ambiguous and recommend a borescope inspection.

================================================================================
SECTION 8: KNOWN MODEL LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

8.1 MEAN COLLAPSE
    Sequence models trained on this dataset can converge to predicting the
    population mean RUL rather than tracking individual engine trajectories.
    The symptom is a near-constant prediction across engines with very
    different true RUL values. This is an architectural limitation, not a data
    defect: it reproduces identically under both symmetric and asymmetric loss
    functions.

8.2 MITIGATIONS
    Two mitigations are known to help:
      - Clip the training target at a ceiling, for example min(RUL, 125), so
        the model is not asked to distinguish 200 from 250 cycles when both
        are far from failure.
      - Replace the recurrent encoder with a temporal convolutional network or
        a small Transformer encoder, which handle long-range dependencies
        without the vanishing-gradient behaviour of a plain LSTM.

8.3 WINDOWING
    Training windows must never cross an engine boundary. A window that spans
    two engines presents the model with a physically impossible transition and
    degrades the learned representation. Build windows per engine.
